## Introduction

Underwater robots, divers, and marine scientists need to grab objects that are wet, rough, or curved, such as rocks, ship hulls, and delicate specimens. Conventional adhesives lose strength in water, and suction cups fail on rough surfaces.

Octopuses solve this problem with suckers that grip almost any surface and let go instantly. Our objective was to build an **octopus-inspired adhesive** that attaches strongly to challenging underwater surfaces and **switches** between holding and releasing on command.

## Methods

I **designed, fabricated, and tested** adhesive prototypes in the Soft Materials and Structures Lab. I fabricated the soft structures by mold casting and then measured their attachment on a range of underwater surfaces, comparing the attached and released states.

The prototypes demonstrated switchable attachment to challenging underwater surfaces. I presented the work as a poster, *"Octopus-Inspired Adhesives with Switchable Attachment to Challenging Underwater Surfaces,"* at the **Adhesion Society Annual Meeting 2025**.

## Discussion

A switchable underwater adhesive could let soft grippers pick up delicate marine specimens without damaging them and then release them cleanly. This project taught me to move a bio-inspired idea from concept to tested prototype and to communicate the results to a professional research audience.
